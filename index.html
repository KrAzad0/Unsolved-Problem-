OP-001Quantum biology

Quantum dynamics in a simplified biological environment

When can a microscopic transfer process retain a measurable quantum signature in a fluctuating, warm molecular environment?

Research outline ↗

Starting model
Begin with a two-state proton-transfer model coupled to a phenomenological noisy environment. Treat the surroundings classically unless the model justifies a quantum bath.

Next investigation
Compare coherent and dissipative dynamics; assess tunnelling, purity, and parameter sensitivity.

OP-002Quantum biology

Isotope effects in a fluctuating double well

How do molecular barrier fluctuations and isotopic mass influence transfer dynamics and observable isotope effects?

Research outline ↗

Starting model
Use an asymmetric double-well potential V(x) = a(x² − b²)² + ΔV x with proton and deuteron masses.

Next investigation
Calculate eigenstate splitting, populations, tunnelling times, and noise-dependent isotope effects.

OP-003Quantum mechanics

A hydrogen atom inside a toroidal cavity

How do toroidal confinement and an ultrashort applied field modify bound-state dynamics of a Coulomb system?

Research outline ↗

Starting model
Solve the Coulomb Schrödinger equation on a solid toroidal domain with hard-wall boundary conditions; add a time-dependent driving term.

Next investigation
Benchmark stationary states, verify convergence, and then study pulse-driven probability densities and observables.

Numerical study
OP-004Mathematical physics

Quantum dynamics with a moving boundary

What changes when the confining boundary of a hydrogen-like system moves periodically in time?

Research outline ↗

Starting model
Consider a time-dependent cavity scale R(t) = R₀[1 + ε cos(Ωt)] and map the moving domain into fixed coordinates.

Next investigation
Derive transformed operators and boundary conditions; compare with known Floquet descriptions and the static limit.

OP-005Ultrafast physics

Ultrafast control of Dirac quasiparticles

Research outline ↗

Research practice

Questions are not claims.

These entries are exploratory directions, not declarations of new discoveries or established open problems. Every project should begin with a literature review, explicit assumptions and a mathematically consistent model.

Next come limiting-case checks, numerical convergence, observable predictions, reproducibility and comparison with published work. The labels above track progress in the notebook; they do not imply peer review.
